# Forensic Ballistics

The scientific study of projectiles in motion and the traces they leave.

## Three branches

- **Internal ballistics** — everything between primer ignition and the bullet leaving the muzzle. Chamber pressure, barrel length, twist rate.
- **External ballistics** — bullet in flight. Trajectory, air resistance, drop.
- **Terminal ballistics** — bullet interacting with the target. Wounding, penetration, deformation.

## Firearm identification

Every firearm imparts unique marks on the bullet and cartridge case:

- **Bullet** — land and groove impressions from rifling; often 4–6 lands.
- **Cartridge case** — firing pin impression, breech-face marks, extractor and ejector marks.

Comparison microscopy is used to match test-fires against evidence specimens. Automated systems (IBIS-style) provide screening candidates but a human examiner still confirms.

## Gunshot residue (GSR)

Particles of primer (Pb, Ba, Sb) and propellant burn products deposited on the shooter's hands, target surface, and nearby items. **SEM-EDX** on the resulting particles is the confirmatory technique.

## Range of fire

Determined by residue pattern:
- **Contact** — laceration and searing.
- **Close (<60 cm)** — powder tattooing/stippling.
- **Intermediate** — soot only.
- **Distant** — no residue.
