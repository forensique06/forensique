# Fingerprints & Biometric Systems

## Ridge patterns

Three major categories from Henry classification:

- **Loops** (~65% of prints) — ulnar or radial, one delta.
- **Whorls** (~30%) — plain, central pocket, double loop, accidental. Two deltas.
- **Arches** (~5%) — plain or tented. No delta.

## Three levels of detail

1. **Level 1** — overall ridge flow and pattern class. Useful for exclusion, not for individualisation.
2. **Level 2** — minutiae: bifurcations, ridge endings, dots. This is the level used for individualisation in classical comparison work.
3. **Level 3** — ridge shape, pores, incipient ridges. Extremely fine, requires high-resolution imaging; supplements Level 2 in difficult cases.

## Development techniques

- **Porous surfaces (paper, cardboard)** — ninhydrin (reacts with amino acids, purple colour), DFO, physical developer.
- **Non-porous surfaces (glass, metal)** — cyanoacrylate fuming (superglue), followed by dye staining or powder.
- **Fluorescent techniques** — used with alternate light sources for prints on difficult backgrounds.

## AFIS

Automated Fingerprint Identification System — algorithms extract minutiae, encode them as vectors, and query against large databases (NAFIS in India). Human examiner still confirms every candidate match.

## Modern biometrics

Same conceptual pipeline as fingerprints, applied to iris, retina, face, voice, gait. Modality trade-offs: iris is extremely accurate but expensive; face is convenient but easier to spoof; voice is remote-friendly but low-security.
