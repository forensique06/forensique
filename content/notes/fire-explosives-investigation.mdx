# Fire, Explosives & Investigation

## The fire triangle

Fire needs three things: **fuel**, **oxygen**, **heat**. Remove any leg and combustion stops. Some texts extend to a fire *tetrahedron* by adding the sustained chain reaction as a fourth element.

## Arson indicators

An arson investigator is looking for evidence that the fire was set deliberately rather than accidental. Common indicators:

- Multiple, unconnected points of origin.
- Accelerant patterns (irregular pour patterns, low burn on floors).
- Trailers connecting rooms.
- Signs of tampering with detectors, alarms, sprinklers.

## Accelerant residue

Debris from suspected origin points is collected in vapour-tight containers and analysed by **GC-MS** for volatile organic compounds — petroleum distillates, alcohols, oxygenates.

## Explosives classification

- **Low-order** (deflagration, <340 m/s): black powder, smokeless powder.
- **High-order** (detonation, >1000 m/s): TNT, RDX, PETN, HMX; nitroglycerin-based dynamites.
- **Primary** — sensitive, used as initiators (lead azide, mercury fulminate).
- **Secondary** — main charges; need an initiator.

## Post-blast investigation

Swabs and debris analysed for residue by GC-MS, HPLC, IC (ion chromatography for oxidisers). Fragment reconstruction can also reveal device components — timers, wiring, container material.
