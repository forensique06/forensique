# Forensic Medicine & Anthropology

## Cause vs manner of death

**Cause** — the disease, injury, or abnormality that produced the death (e.g. gunshot wound to head).
**Manner** — the way it came about: natural, accident, suicide, homicide, or undetermined.

Cause is a medical finding; manner is a legal/investigative determination the pathologist contributes to.

## The autopsy

**External examination** — clothing, marks, injuries, lividity, rigor, decomposition state.
**Internal examination** — Y or T incision, organ-by-organ dissection, weighing, histology sampling.
**Ancillary** — toxicology, histopathology, microbiology as indicated.

## Time since death (post-mortem interval)

Multiple concurrent methods:

- **Rigor mortis** — sets in ~2–6 h, fully developed ~12 h, resolves ~24–48 h.
- **Livor mortis** — visible within 30 min, fixed after ~8–12 h.
- **Algor mortis** — body cools ~1°C per hour (variable).
- **Vitreous humour potassium** — rises linearly for ~100 h; more reliable than rigor for later intervals.
- **Entomology** — successional insect fauna, larval age.

## Skeletal remains

Forensic anthropology addresses:

- **Species** — human vs non-human.
- **Sex** — pelvis (most reliable), skull (secondary).
- **Age** — sutures, dental eruption, epiphyseal fusion, pubic symphysis.
- **Stature** — long bone regression equations (Trotter and Gleser).
- **Ancestry** — cranial and dental morphology.

## Injury interpretation

Blunt-force, sharp-force, gunshot, thermal, chemical — pattern, direction, ante-mortem vs post-mortem, and correlation with the scene account.
