# Analytical Techniques

Every downstream forensic discipline eventually hands its physical evidence to an instrument. This unit is about those instruments — the ones that separate, identify, and quantify substances.

## Chromatography

Separation of a mixture by differential partitioning between a stationary phase and a mobile phase.

- **Gas chromatography (GC)** — volatile analytes carried by an inert gas through a coated capillary column. Common in toxicology, arson accelerant detection, drug identification.
- **HPLC** — high-pressure liquid chromatography for non-volatile / thermally unstable compounds. Common in drug and toxicology work.
- **TLC** — thin-layer chromatography. Cheap, quick, qualitative screening.

## Spectroscopy

- **UV-Vis** — quantitative absorbance in the ultraviolet/visible range. Widely used in preliminary drug screens.
- **IR (FTIR)** — molecular fingerprint from vibrational modes. Excellent for identifying polymers, paints, unknown solids.
- **AAS / AES / ICP** — atomic spectroscopy for elemental analysis, especially heavy metals in toxicology and GSR.

## Mass Spectrometry

Ionises molecules and separates them by mass-to-charge ratio. When coupled to a chromatograph (GC-MS or LC-MS), it gives both separation and identification in one run. This is the gold-standard confirmatory technique in modern forensic toxicology and drug analysis.

## Microscopy

- **Stereo** — 3D low-magnification view for evidence sorting.
- **Compound optical** — hair, fibre, trace evidence morphology.
- **Comparison microscope** — two specimens side-by-side; core tool for ballistics and toolmark work.
- **SEM / SEM-EDX** — surface morphology at nanometre scale plus elemental analysis. GSR particles, glass, paint layers.
