# Fundamentals of Forensic Science

## What forensic science actually is

Forensic science is the application of scientific methods and techniques to legal problems — most commonly, criminal investigations. It sits at the intersection of natural science, law, and investigation, and its job is to produce evidence that a court can act on.

## The Locard Exchange Principle

The foundational idea, articulated by **Edmond Locard** in the early 20th century, is that *every contact leaves a trace*. When two objects come into contact, material transfers between them — hair, fibre, skin cells, glass fragments, tool marks. This transfer is bidirectional and, in principle, always detectable given a sensitive enough method.

Locard's principle is why every other forensic discipline exists. Fingerprints, DNA, ballistics, trace evidence — all of them are practical applications of *find the transfer*.

## Brief history worth remembering for MCQs

- **Mathieu Orfila** (1813) — often called the father of modern toxicology.
- **Alphonse Bertillon** (1879) — anthropometry, the first systematic identification method.
- **Francis Galton / Edward Henry** (1892 / 1897) — fingerprint classification.
- **Karl Landsteiner** (1901) — ABO blood groups.
- **Calvin Goddard** (1920s) — comparison microscope for ballistics.
- **Alec Jeffreys** (1984) — DNA fingerprinting.

## Chain of custody

Every piece of forensic evidence must have an unbroken, documented chain of custody from the moment it is collected at the scene to the moment it is presented in court. A break in that chain is one of the most common reasons evidence is excluded, regardless of how strong the science is.
