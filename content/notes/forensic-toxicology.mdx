# Forensic Toxicology

The branch that answers *what was in this person / this sample*, in what quantity, and (sometimes) *how did it get there and did it contribute to death*.

## Classification of poisons

- **Corrosives** — strong acids/alkalis; local injury.
- **Irritants** — inorganic (arsenic, mercury) or organic (aconite, castor).
- **Neurotics** — cerebral (opium, alcohol), spinal (strychnine), peripheral (curare).
- **Cardiac** — digitalis, oleander.
- **Asphyxiants** — CO, HCN, H2S.

## ADME

Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion — every toxicology question ultimately reduces to where in this cycle the analyte was at the time of sampling.

## Common analytes and biological samples

- **Ethanol** — blood, breath, urine.
- **Opioids / benzodiazepines** — urine screen, blood confirmation, hair for chronic use.
- **Heavy metals** — hair and nail for chronic exposure; urine for recent.
- **Volatile inhalants** — blood only, tightly sealed.

## Screening vs confirmation

Toxicology labs use immunoassay for cheap, sensitive screening, then confirm every presumptive positive with a mass-spectrometric method (typically GC-MS or LC-MS/MS). A pure screen result is never a legal finding on its own.
